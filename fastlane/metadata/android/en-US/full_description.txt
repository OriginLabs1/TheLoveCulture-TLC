TLC records recognition — who showed up for whom, and what they did — in a record that cannot be quietly rewritten, and that survives closing the app.

It exists for one narrow purpose: to make care and contribution visible between people who trust each other, and to keep the memory of it beyond the reach of a silent edit.

TOKENS, RECEIPTS AND FACES

• One Tokens surface for everything that moves: Support, Pioneer's Token and Growth Grant, with an All / Offered / Received segment and an honest timeline from offer to acknowledgement.
• Circulation counters for offered, transferred and received. They describe movement only: there is no balance, no ranking and no pooled total.
• Receipt images you own. Attach a photo or screenshot to a token, replace it, remove it, or open it full size. The image is downscaled, re-encoded and stripped of metadata before it is stored in the app's private folder; the record keeps a file name and a fingerprint, never the bytes.
• Visibility is explicit and revocable: Only me, This transfer, My circle, Anyone with the link, or Public in Record. Sharing opens the system chooser and nothing leaves until you choose to send it.
• Forest green marks received, verified and growth; royal purple marks Pioneer, stewardship and recognition. Both are accents only, and every state is also spelled out in words.
• Motion is selective: counters animate, cards reveal, taps respond — and turning motion off removes the animation without removing information.

THE DOOR, THE FACE AND THE ROUTE

• A distinct username and a word/phrase passphrase open the circle. Four ordinary words or one long line; a strength guide shows the shape without shaming. A salt and a PBKDF2 verifier stay on the device, and the passphrase is never stored, logged or sent.
• No biometrics. Ever. There is no fingerprint prompt, no face prompt, no passkey sheet and no device-credential fallback. The passphrase is the only door, and a build gate fails on any biometric or passkey API.
• Lock now returns to the passphrase screen. Sign out returns to the door and leaves the record on the device. A forgotten passphrase can be reset only by starting a fresh circle on this device; export a bundle first if you want to keep the record. A device with an older record but no passphrase is asked once to set the words, without discarding it.
• An avatar studio frames your photo: drag to move, pinch or use the slider to zoom, reset, then save the exact circle shown. The preview and the saved file share one geometry, so a head is no longer cropped off by a silent centre crop. With no photo, an initials monogram sits on a quiet gradient.
• A profile that reads like a person: a gradient hero, an overlapping ringed avatar, an edit badge, three honest counters, your details, your photo, your presence summary and the security of the door in one calm column.
• Presence is four independent choices: Online now, Last seen, Typing and Read receipts. Each starts at Nobody and can separately be Nobody, Selected people, My circle or Everyone. Invisible overrides everything. Selected and hidden people are explicit, exact last seen is its own choice, and a live preview shows what one person would see. Silence is never rendered as offline.
• A token transfer asks for the real Transfer Method while you are sending: provider, fee, who pays, arrival, limit and availability, with a two-minute held window and a one-tap switch. Direct bank and instant bank rail are usable today; card-funded, cash-agent and international routes are shown disabled with the reason rather than invented.
• TLC never holds or moves the money. You complete the route in your own bank or licensed provider and bring back the reference.

TRUST AND CONTACT

• Handles give every person an address. Your profile gets a @handle at onboarding, validated locally. @pioneer is the official role and @sircollins is the person behind it; both are reserved, and both reach the same desk.
• People is a small, deliberate address book. Add the people you actually support, give them a handle and a role, and attach one saved destination.
• The Bank vault saves a destination once. The ten-digit account number is validated locally against its NUBAN check digit, encrypted with AES-256-GCM and a key held by the Android Keystore, and never written to the state, the record, a backup or a log. The app shows only the bank name, the last four digits and a fingerprint. Revealing the full number is an explicit action; it hides again on its own and the reveal is written to the record.
• Send Support is the direct peer flow: Supportive or Recognition support to a person you know, with an optional amount and a reference. TLC records the offer, the claim and the acknowledgement. A claim is a statement, not a settlement.
• Pioneer's Token replaces the old debt-like label. Activation, Reactivation, Courtesy and Joy go directly to the Concept Pioneer's own account. TLC records the intent and the evidence; it never holds the money. The destination stays in the vault and is revealed only to the payer, only after the token exists.
• The Pioneer Desk is purpose-first contact. Choose why you are writing, see the opening before it leaves the app, then pick a channel: Telegram, email or the website. WhatsApp is deferred because the founder's Telegram already uses that number. The local contact log keeps only the fact of contact — purpose, channel and time — never the message.
• The Contact & Consent Framework is doctrine in this build. The public wording that describes it is under legal review and may still change.

A NEW SURFACE

• No pink. Royal blue, azure, silver-grey and cool white surfaces, with midnight navy for the icon tile.
• A new emblem: a crowned heart with a sprout, drawn as a vector so one master serves the launcher, the themed icon and the store listing.
• Four tabs — Today, Record, People, Settings — plus a Create sheet for every everyday verb.
• A dark theme that is midnight ink, not black; motion that respects the system setting; haptics that can be turned off.

THE WHOLE SHAPE, IN ONE BUILD

• Onboarding asks for a name, a role and a handle only after it has explained the promise: local by default, hash-chained, non-custodial.
• Today is the home surface: your circle, whether the record is intact, the everyday verbs, every open loop and the modules that make up TLC.
• The Record is searchable and filterable, and any entry opens to its Merkle proof against the current root. A filter is a reading aid, never a change to the record.
• Growth Grants record a witnessed pledge: the beneficiary, the purpose, the evidence you actually have, the person who verified the milestone, and the bank reference when the transfer is done.
• Circle holds a Primary Steward and a Co-Steward. Either seat can veto; a sensitive decision needs both signatures to close. This build uses local personas, not accounts — cross-device invitations and signed consent grants arrive with sync.
• Settings carries theme, motion, haptics, Receptive Mode, the trust and contact surfaces, record export, a verified backup bundle, restore, and seal sharing.
• Verify recomputes the chain, shows the head hash and Merkle root, and exports the exact text the ledger-verify command-line tool reads.

WHAT THE RECORD DOES

• It keeps your record on the device. Entries are written to a readable text file in the app's private folder and flushed to disk before the app says they were kept.
• It re-checks the whole record on every launch. Each entry carries the SHA-256 hash of the one before it, so editing a past entry breaks every later hash.
• It names the damage. A tail lost on this device is reported as missing entries; an edited line is reported as a failed verification. A record that does not verify opens read-only instead of quietly accepting new history.
• It repairs an interrupted write by dropping only the incomplete line, then verifying what remains.
• It refuses a damaged state file or backup bundle rather than silently replacing or merging it.

THE SHARED PLANE, LIVE

• A fifth Live tab connects to a thin, self-hostable TLC relay for one-to-one messages and presence. It is optional: the private record, vault, drafts, photos and receipts still work in airplane mode.
• Messages are sealed on the device with P-256 ECDH, HKDF-SHA256 and AES-256-GCM, with a SHA256withECDSA sender signature. Keys are wrapped by an Android Keystore AES-256-GCM key. The relay stores ciphertext, a conversation id, timing and size — never message text. This composes audited JCA primitives directly, so it is labelled E2EE alpha, not a formally audited ratchet.
• Presence is filtered on the relay against your consent record. Online now, last seen, typing and read receipts each have an audience, and a signal you did not share is sent as "not shared" rather than guessed as offline.
• Register a handle and device key, accept or block connection requests, and send with queued, sent, delivered and read states. A changed device key raises a warning instead of being accepted silently.
• Delete the account from the Live tab. The relay removes the account record, its connections and queued ciphertext. Undelivered ciphertext is pruned at 30 days regardless.
• Pear companion: export a record-shape-only handoff and replicate it with a Holepunch/Hyperswarm node over the public DHT. The handoff carries no subjects, amounts, photos, receipts or vault fields, and the peer layer has no authority over a shared fact.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO YET

There is no cross-device merge of the private record, no calls and no remote Pioneer attestation desk. The relay is a courier, not an authority. Transfer Method execution through a licensed provider is the next milestone. A Co-Steward is a local persona, not a remote authenticated person. The Received view shows only what is on this device and does not invent a number. Encrypted messaging is E2EE alpha, and an audited ratcheting protocol is future work. Running the Pear node is a separate choice and does not by itself sync the private record.

WHAT TLC WILL NEVER DO

• It never holds, moves, pools or escrows money. There is no wallet, no balance, no payment rail, no card data.
• It never offers a fingerprint, face, passkey or device-credential door. Your passphrase is the only key.
• The app shows where to send a transfer and records the evidence you bring back. The money moves between two private bank accounts, bank to bank, and TLC is not a party to it.
• Recognition tokens are signals of appreciation: not currency, not transferable, not redeemable, not crypto.
• No analytics on amounts, no advertising identifier, no third-party tracking.

PRIVATE BY CONSTRUCTION

The private record, vault, drafts, photos and receipts stay on the device. The APK requests INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE for the relay only; it requests no storage, contacts, location, camera or microphone permission. Cleartext HTTP is disabled and a network security config pins TLS. The relay processes handles, connection state, timing, sizes and IP addresses; it never receives your passphrase, message text, the vault or the ledger. The full bank account number stays inside the encrypted vault and is never copied into the record, a backup, a log or a notification. There is no Google Play Services, no Firebase, no analytics and no proprietary SDK.

CHECK IT YOURSELF

Export the record, then run the ledger-verify command-line tool from the source repository. It recomputes every hash and reports the first break — the same check the app performs, outside the app.

WHO IT IS FOR

Families, small circles, community groups and cooperatives — anyone who wants a trustworthy record of who showed up for whom, without handing that record to a company.

FREE AND OPEN SOURCE

GPL-3.0-or-later, no proprietary SDKs, no Google Play Services. The same signed build is published to GitHub Releases, F-Droid and Orion Store, and uploaded unchanged to Uptodown.
