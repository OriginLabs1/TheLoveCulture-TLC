TLC 0.0.4 — the whole shape of it.

v0.0.3 made the record survive. v0.0.4 makes the rest of TLC visible and usable: a first-run
onboarding that explains what this is before it asks for anything, a home surface that shows every
open loop, Growth Grants with their evidence and witness trail, Pioneer dues that stop at proof, a
two-seat Circle, preferences, verified backup and restore, and an About screen that states plainly
what exists now and what does not.

Nothing in this build moves money. The doctrine is unchanged: non-custodial, local-first, no wallet,
no balance, no escrow. Recognition tokens are signals of appreciation — not currency, not
transferable, not redeemable, not crypto.

### What is new

- **Onboarding, on your terms.** Three short screens: what TLC is, what each role means, and the
  name this device will greet you with. Nothing is written until the final tap, and the local-only
  promise is on the first screen rather than in a policy nobody opens.
- **Today.** One calm home surface: your circle and role, whether the record is intact, the three
  everyday verbs (recognise, pledge, record a due), every open loop with a count, and the modules
  that make up TLC. Every block leads somewhere real.
- **Journal, made readable.** Search, filters for recognition, grants, dues and governance, an
  entry-by-entry Merkle proof sheet, and a footer that shows the seal you can share. Filtering only
  changes the view, never the record.
- **Growth Grants.** Pledge support to a named beneficiary, attach the evidence you actually have,
  record who witnessed the milestone, and note the bank reference when the transfer is done. The
  lifecycle is a total function over (stage, event): a grant cannot claim a stage the evidence does
  not support. The money still moves directly between two private bank accounts.
- **Pioneer dues, without pretending.** Record a platform due, issue the instruction, and submit the
  proof you brought back. The app stops there: it does not attest a Pioneer payment locally, because
  only the Pioneer can do that. The awaiting state says so.
- **Circle with two seats.** Name a Co-Steward, open a sensitive decision, and see both signatures
  as they arrive. Either seat can veto. A proposal needs both signatures to close. This build is
  explicit that these are local personas, not accounts: cross-device invitations and signed consent
  grants arrive with sync.
- **Settings that belong to you.** Light, dark or system theme; motion and haptics; Receptive Mode;
  export the record, export a verified bundle, restore a bundle, share a seal, and open verification.
- **Backup and restore, proven.** The state file is written atomically; a bundle carries the record
  and the state together and is refused if either does not verify. Restore never merges silently.
- **About, written honestly.** Purpose, the module map, what this build does, what is still absent,
  version, licence and source. No roadmap disguised as a feature list.

### What has not changed

- **The record is still the record.** Append-only, hash-chained, `fsync`ed before the app says it was
  kept, re-verified on every launch, and read-only when it does not check out. A damaged state file
  is refused rather than quietly replaced.
- **Still no network.** The APK requests no `INTERNET` permission and contains no Play Services, no
  Firebase, no analytics and no proprietary SDK. Nothing leaves the device unless you export or share
  it.
- **Still no custody.** No wallet, no balance, no payment rail, no card data. The app records the
  intent, the instruction and the evidence; the transfer happens bank to bank.

### What this build does not do yet

There is no account, no sync and no multi-device merge. A Co-Steward is a local persona, not a
remote authenticated person. The remote Pioneer attestation desk, cross-device invitations and
signed consent grants are the next milestone. This is a preview of the whole shape, not a claim that
the network layer exists.

### Proven by the build, not by the README

- **112 invariant tests** across the domain state machine, the ledger, the file format, crash
  recovery, the experience layer and the command-line verifier.
- Simulated disk failures, torn writes, deleted tails, edited amounts, rewritten hashes, duplicated
  idempotency keys, invalid state files and damaged backups are all covered by tests.
- The `foss` channel is checked for Play Services, Firebase and tracking dependencies.
- The release APK is checked for the absence of the `INTERNET` permission and of Play Services
  classes in its dex, and its signing certificate is pinned to the project's key.

### Where it ships

The same signed `tlc-0.0.4-foss.apk` is published to GitHub Releases and submitted to F-Droid and
Orion Store. Uptodown is a manual upload through its developer console of that same APK. No store
re-signs it: one package id, one signature, one update path.

GPL-3.0-or-later. No analytics, no advertising identifier, no third-party tracking.
