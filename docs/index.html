TLC is a local-first, non-custodial record of care and contribution. It keeps a tamper-evident journal for families, circles, community groups and cooperatives — and now adds a thin, self-hostable relay for live encrypted messages and consented presence. The journal stays on your device; the relay is a courier, never a company holding the memory.

What this build does

  • Live now. A fifth Live tab connects to a self-hostable TLC relay. One-to-one messages are sealed on the device with P-256 ECDH + HKDF-SHA256 + AES-256-GCM and a sender signature; the relay carries ciphertext only. E2EE alpha, not a formally audited ratchet.
  • Presence on the internet, on your terms. Online now, last seen, typing and read receipts are four separate signals. A signal you hide is sent as "not shared", never guessed as offline.
  • A real account you can delete. Register a handle and device key, accept or block connection requests, send and receive messages with queued/sent/delivered/read states. Deleting the account removes the relay record, its connections and queued ciphertext.
  • Pear companion. Export a record-shape-only handoff and replicate it with a Holepunch/Hyperswarm node over the public DHT. No subjects, amounts, photos, receipts or vault fields; no authority over a shared fact.
  • A distinct username and a word/phrase passphrase open the circle. No fingerprint, face, passkey or device shortcut — the passphrase is the only door.
  • An avatar studio: pick a photo, then drag, pinch and zoom to frame the exact circle that is saved. With no photo, an initials monogram replaces the empty box.
  • Transfer Methods inside a token transfer: real routes with provider, fee, payer, arrival, limit and availability, plus a two-minute held window. TLC never holds or moves the money.
  • Onboarding, a local profile and a @handle, then Today, Record, People, Tokens, Live, Settings and a Create sheet.
  • An append-only, hash-chained record that is re-verified on every launch and opens read-only if it does not check out.
  • One Tokens surface for every Support, Pioneer's Token and Growth Grant, with circulation counters and an honest timeline.
  • Receipt images you own: downscaled, metadata-stripped, stored privately, and shared only with an audience you choose.
  • Names for people and roles: @pioneer is the official role and @sircollins is the person behind it.
  • An encrypted bank vault: save a destination once, see only the last four digits, and reveal the full number only on purpose.
  • Send Support, Pioneer's Token, a purpose-first Pioneer Desk, Growth Grants with evidence and witness stages.
  • Verified backup and restore, Merkle proofs per entry, and an export the ledger-verify tool reads.

What it will never do

TLC is an instrument, not a bank. It never holds, pools, escrows or moves money. There is no wallet, no balance, no payment rail in this build and no card data. A transfer happens bank to bank between two private accounts; TLC records the intent, the instruction and the evidence.

A full bank account number is never compiled in and never enters the record. It lives only in the payer's encrypted vault, protected by the Android Keystore; the app shows the bank name, the last four digits and a fingerprint, and reveals the rest only on an explicit action.

What v0.0.8 does not claim

The relay is a courier, not a bank and not an authority over the private record. The ledger, vault, drafts, photos and receipts stay on the device and work in airplane mode. Transfer Method execution through a licensed provider, cross-device merge, calls and the remote Pioneer attestation desk are the next milestone. Messages are sealed with audited JCA primitives composed directly, so the app labels them E2EE alpha; a formally audited messaging protocol is future work. The Pear node is an optional companion, never the authority for a shared fact.

Presence is filtered on the relay against your consent record. The relay necessarily sees handles, connection state, timing, sizes and IP addresses; it never sees message text or the private record. Undelivered ciphertext is pruned at 30 days, and deleting your account deletes it sooner.
